Threshold Prototype v0.1

A tenancy risk score nobody can explain is not a product.

Threshold flags households at risk of losing their tenancy, early enough to do something about it. Every flag arrives with the signals behind it, what changed this week, and one suggested action — with the evidence for it.

The explanation is not a feature bolted on for the DPIA. It is the reason the model was built the way it was.

01 / INTERPRETABLE BY CONSTRUCTION

Not a black box with an apology attached

An additive model with monotonic shape functions, chosen over gradient boosting on purpose. Contributions are the decision rather than a reconstruction of it. In a DPIA, that is a different conversation.

02 / CHANGE, NOT LEVEL

Early warning is a derivative problem

£900 in arrears and stable is a different case from £300 that moved £280 in five weeks. Threshold sorts by movement, and every flag says what changed since the last run.

03 / IT ENDS IN A DECISION

A score with no next step is just more work

Each flag terminates in one suggested action matched to the dominant signal cluster, with the observed effect in similar cases — and a reject-with-reason button that feeds the model review.

Signal library — what the criteria actually mean

Every signal the model can see, what it does to the score, where the data comes from, the lawful basis for using it, and how long it lasts. The curve on each card is the model's real shape function — swept from the live scoring code, not drawn by hand. This screen is the DPIA annex and the officer training material at the same time, which is why it is a screen and not a PDF.
How to read a card
The curve maps the signal's value (left to right, low to high) onto its contribution in log-odds (vertical). Flat sections mean the model has deliberately stopped responding — a household four times over on arrears is not treated as twice the risk of one twice over, because that is not what the data shows and it is not how anyone should be treated. Every curve is monotonic: a signal can only ever push one way, so a signal can never surprise an officer by reversing.
Signals deliberately excluded
Ethnicity, nationality, religion, disability, age, sex, household composition. Not model inputs, and not proxies for model inputs. Held separately for fairness monitoring only — see Assurance.

Individual-level deprivation or income data. Area deprivation appears as context and contributes nothing to a score. The fuel-poverty signal is derived from the property — wall construction and EPC — never from the household.

Anything predicting whether recovery action would succeed. That is a different model with a different purpose, and building it inside this one is how a support tool quietly becomes an enforcement tool.

Horizon — what a future model looks like

Everything on the other screens is what a first version can defend today. This screen is the argument for where it goes next — and the honest version of that argument is that predicting who will fail is the easy half. The hard and more useful half is predicting who a visit would actually change.
Capability roadmap
Each stage is gated on the one before it producing enough outcome data to train the next. Skipping a stage means building the clever thing on evidence that does not exist yet.

v1 · REACTIVE

Signals and explanation

Additive risk model over operational and property signals. Exact contributions, suggested actions from a fixed library, full audit trail. Defensible today with data the landlord already holds.

Live · needs no new data sharing
v2 · TRAJECTORY

Paths, not points

Model the household's path over time rather than its state this morning. Recognise recurring shapes — the post-migration dip, the slow drift, the sudden break — and forecast where the trajectory lands, with an interval.

6–12 months · needs 18 months of scored history
v3 · EFFECT

Predict the intervention, not the failure

Learn the effect of each action from recorded outcomes. Move the suggested action from a rule mapped to a cluster to an estimate of what this specific action would do for this specific household.

12–24 months · needs consistent outcome recording
v4 · UPLIFT

Who does help actually help?

Causal uplift modelling: rank by expected change in outcome rather than by risk. Requires deliberate randomisation in who gets contacted first — the ethical and operational design of that is harder than the maths.

24 months+ · needs an ethics case and a trial design

Outcomes — what actually happened

Two halves, and the second one is the uncomfortable half. Did the interventions work? — the outcome of every actioned case, which is the data the whole roadmap depends on and which no product collects by accident. And what did we miss? — the tenancies that failed with no flag, or with a flag that arrived too late to matter. A model assessed only on the cases it flagged is marking its own homework.

Assurance & governance

Model card
Purpose
Identify households at elevated risk of tenancy failure early enough for a supportive intervention to change the outcome.
Output
Probability of a sustainment failure event within 90 days. Event = NOSP served, arrears > 8× weekly rent, abandonment, or surrender under arrears.
Model form
Additive logistic model with monotonic, bounded shape functions per signal. Chosen over gradient boosting specifically so that reported contributions are exact rather than approximated. No signal may contribute more than 35% of total risk.
Training population
Synthetic. In production: 5 years of general-needs tenancies, excluding supported and temporary accommodation.
Measured performance
Recall 58% — of 19 tenancy failures in 12 months, 11 were flagged Red or Amber with 30+ days of usable notice, 3 were flagged too late to matter, and 5 were never flagged. This is the number that matters and it is deliberately the one published first. Precision on flagged cases is higher and less informative. Full case-by-case review on the Outcomes screen.
Known limitations
Under-performs on tenancies < 8 weeks old (abstains). Sensitive to rent-account data quality. Area deprivation is context only and is excluded from individual-level decisions. Holds no signal for relationship breakdown or household composition change — a known blind spot, evidenced by the missed-case review, and one whose fix is not obviously proportionate.
Explicitly out of scope
Not an arrears recovery or enforcement prioritisation tool.
Not an input to allocations, transfers, or tenancy renewal.
Not a fraud detection system.
Not automated — no contact, escalation or action occurs without an officer action.
Fairness monitoring
Flag rate and precision by characteristic, against the population reference line. Sustained divergence triggers a model review.
Testing for disparate impact requires holding demographic data. It is held in a separate controlled store, joined only for aggregate monitoring, accessible to two named roles, and is never a model input. Removing the data would not remove the bias — it would remove the ability to detect it.
Abstention rate
How often the model declines to judge. A rising Grey rate is a data-quality alarm, not a model failure.

DPIA artefacts
Article 22 position
Threshold does not make solely automated decisions producing legal or similarly significant effects. Every output is a prompt to a human, every action requires an officer instruction, and the audit log evidences the human step in each case. The log is the proof, not the policy.
Lawful basis
Tier 1 — performance of the tenancy contract / legitimate interests (LIA on file). Tier 2 — legitimate interests, balancing test per signal. Tier 3 — disabled; requires Art. 9 condition, DPO sign-off and a named justification per case.
Retention
Signals decay on the published schedule shown on every resident record. Scores are not retained beyond 24 months. Arrears from four years ago do not shape a score today.
Data subject rights
DSAR export includes the full contribution breakdown for every score ever produced for that tenancy, in plain English. Contestation route published in the tenant handbook.
Transparency
Resident-facing explanation generated at the same moment as the officer explanation — not reconstructed afterwards on request.
